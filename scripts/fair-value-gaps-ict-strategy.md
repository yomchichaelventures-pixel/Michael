## 1. Intro / Hook

Most fair value gaps are traps. You've lived this. You mark a clean gap. Price taps it. You buy. Then one candle slices through, and your stop is gone. The gap wasn't bad. It was empty. By the end of this video, you'll know which gaps hold, which gaps fail, and the exact price I enter at. Stick around for the end, because one question sits behind every winner here.

## 2. The Setup

Start with the gap itself. A fair value gap is a three-candle pattern. Look at candle one and candle three. If their wicks don't overlap, the space between them is the gap. ICT teaches that this is price delivered in one direction only. One side got filled. The other side didn't. That imbalance tends to get revisited.

Now the order block. It's the last opposing candle before a strong move. For a long, the last down candle before price launches. That's where heavy orders sat. Price often returns to defend them.

Here's what most traders never check. They trade the gap alone. Or the block alone. But a gap sitting on top of an order block is different. Two footprints. One price. The same hands left both marks. When price returns, it meets two reasons to turn at once.

One reason is a hope. Two reasons is a plan.

Overlap alone still isn't enough. The move that built it must be real. I need displacement. Big, full-bodied candles. And I need a break of structure. Price must take out a recent swing point. No displacement, no break, no setup.

So here's the sequence.

One. Liquidity gets swept. Equal lows, an old low, anything with stops resting under it.
Two. Displacement away from the sweep.
Three. A break of structure.
Four. Find the gap that overlaps the order block.
Five. Wait for the first touch.

Now the entry. I don't buy the top edge of the gap. I don't wait for the bottom edge. I use the midpoint. ICT calls it consequent encroachment. The top edge fills too early, and your stop ends up far away. The bottom edge often gets missed. The middle is the balance point. That's where the gap is fairly priced. The block has a midpoint too. ICT calls it the mean threshold. Close to my entry is ideal.

Stop goes beyond the order block. Not inside it. Beyond it. If a body closes past the block, the gap flips. That's an inversion. The idea is dead.

Target is the opposing liquidity. The highs price hasn't touched yet.

Shorts? Same thing, upside down. Buyside gets swept. Price drops hard. The gap sits under the last up candle. Same midpoint. Same stop logic.

Let's go to the chart.

## 8. The One Question / Filter

Now the reveal from the start.

Go back through every winning trade in this video. The New York long. The London short. The twist. Both replays. Before every single entry, something got taken. Equal lows. The Asian high. The London low.

Now look at the losers. The rest-stop gap had nothing behind it. The upper gap in the twist had nothing under it.

That's what an empty gap is. No fuel.

So before every trade, I ask one question. Not whether the gap looks good. Whose stops paid for this move?

If I can point to them on the chart, the gap has a reason to hold. If I can't, I'm guessing.

Nothing taken, no trade.

## 9. The Checklist

One. Liquidity swept first. Stops taken before anything else.
Two. Displacement you can count, candle by candle.
Three. A clear break of structure, not a wick.
Four. A fresh gap overlapping an order block, untouched until now.
Five. Entry at the midpoint, stop beyond the block, target at the opposite pool.

If one is missing, I sit on my hands.
